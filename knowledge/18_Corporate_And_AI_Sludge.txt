Corporate Sludge: Low-Grade Friction Plays
Beyond hiding cancel buttons, corporations run dozens of low-grade, friction-heavy tactics known as sludge—the deliberate addition of cognitive and logistical friction designed to make giving up cheaper than fighting back:

1. Automated Phone Tree Maze: Trapping callers in endless IVR trees with deliberate omission of human agents to deflect 70% of inbound calls ($5–$12 human cost vs. pennies for deflection).
2. Form Letter Mail-In Rebates: Advertising products with rebate discounts while requiring manual UPC barcode cutouts, multi-page PDFs, and 14-day postal windows, counting on 40–60% actuarial slippage.
3. Asymmetric Support Channels: Sign up or upgrade in 30 seconds via app; dispute an unauthorized charge only by sending a certified letter to a distant P.O. Box.
4. Drip Pricing & Junk Surcharges: Drip pricing adding service fees, platform convenience fees, and regulatory surcharges at the final checkout screen after 10 minutes of manual entry.
5. Cookie Consent Obstacle Course: Giant green 'Accept All' button contrasted against a gray 'Manage Preferences' link concealing 85 individual vendor toggles pre-checked under 'Legitimate Interest'.
6. Forced Account Creation: Mandating full app downloads and account registration to perform a 5-second parking payment or view a menu, solely for data harvesting.
7. Unsubscribable Marketing Loops: Requiring manual email entry and imposing 10-day processing delays on unsubscribe requests while presenting ambiguous multi-category checkboxes.

The Sludge Calculation:

- Spending Money / Upgrading: High speed, single tap, zero questions (Immediate cash conversion).
- Getting Help / Canceling / Claiming Refunds: Low speed, redundant loops, manual forms (Surrender via exhaustion).

AI Sludge: The New Frontier of Extraction
AI companies have weaponized unpredictability. They use the inherent randomness of AI generation to drain credits and force upsells.

8. The Suno Generation Funnel: Suno renders a mediocre song based on clear prompts. To get the exact result, the user burns through their free credits with multiple 'tries.' Once out of credits, Suno offers 'Suno Studio'—a premium paid tier—to manually fix the song. They intentionally cripple the AI's first-pass output to drain credits, then charge a subscription to do the manual labor the AI failed to do.
9. The Token Throttle: Chatbots that intentionally cut off long responses mid-sentence or hallucinate on complex tasks, forcing the user to re-prompt and burn through their API token limits or subscription quotas.

Frontline Defense Blueprint:

- Bait-and-Switch: Test export/save functionality with dummy data before investing real work.
- Pre-Checked Annual: Check statement amounts immediately; initiate same-day chargeback/app store refund.
- Trojan Free Trial: Use disposable virtual cards with a $1 spend limit or cancel immediately via account management.
- Forced Degradation: Migrate to open-source or local-first software alternatives that cannot be remotely throttled.
- False Urgency: Refresh in incognito or inspect the timer code to confirm the permanent base price.
- Privacy-for-Cash: Deploy browser containerization, tracker-blocking extensions, and burner credentials.
- AI Sludge: Stop re-prompting. If an AI tool fails 3 times, it is a deliberate design flaw. Switch to a local model or a competitor.
