14. The "Family" Trap (Emotional Exploitation)
The Corporate Line / Verbal Cover: "We're a family here; we look out for each other."
What It Actually Means: Families forgive mistakes, offer unconditional safety, and don't lay you off via automated email to boost quarterly EBITDA. They use "family" as an emotional guilt trip to get free overtime, unpaid coverage, and compliance with bad treatment.
The Trap: The moment you set a healthy boundary (refusing to work on leave, asking for overtime pay), you are framed as "disloyal" or "abandoning the team."
The Counter-Play: Strip away emotion and re-anchor strictly to the commercial contract.
The Script: "I value our strong collaborative working relationship. To ensure I continue delivering sustainable, high-quality work within our operational parameters, I must keep my focus strictly on core contractual deliverables during scheduled hours."

15. The Feedback Ambush (Hoarding the Knife)
    The Corporate Line / Verbal Cover: (After months of "Great job, no complaints" in 1-on-1s) Dropping a massive list of unaddressed "deficiencies" from six months ago during annual review.
    What It Actually Means: If an issue actually impacted the business, a competent manager would correct it immediately. They intentionally hoard feedback to justify denying you a raise, title bump, or bonus.
    The Trap: You can't defend against events that happened 180 days ago because memories fade and context is lost. They win the narrative by default.
    The Counter-Play: Procedural challenge on managerial competence and mandatory real-time logging.
    The Script: "As these items were not raised during our weekly 1-on-1s on [Date A] or [Date B] when operational correction was possible, let's establish a documented protocol moving forward: any operational variance must be logged and shared within 48 hours so it can be rectified in real time."
16. The Moving Goalpost (The Sisyphean Promotion)
    The Corporate Line / Verbal Cover: "You hit the metrics, but we now need to see more 'executive presence' / 'strategic maturity' / 'leadership alignment.'"
    What It Actually Means: They never intended to promote you. They wanted the extra labor without paying the executive rate. By shifting the criteria to subjective, unmeasurable buzzwords, they can keep you chasing the prize indefinitely.
    The Trap: You exhaust yourself trying to fulfill a requirement that has no clear definition, guaranteeing you will never reach it.
    The Counter-Play: Pin subjective terms down to mathematical baselines and objective scorecards.
    The Script: "To ensure objective alignment across leadership, what specific, measurable behaviors or business outcomes define 'strategic maturity' for this role, and what objective scorecard will be used to verify them next quarter?"
17. The Unofficial "Acting" Promotion (Free Labor Pipeline)
    The Corporate Line / Verbal Cover: "We'd like you to step up into the interim lead role—it's a huge opportunity to prove yourself."
    What It Actually Means: They freeze the hiring budget and leave you "acting" in the senior position for 9 to 18 months at your junior pay rate. If the project stumbles, they blame you; if it succeeds, they pocket the payroll savings.
    The Trap: They exploit your ambition and indomitable work ethic to subsidize corporate overhead.
    The Counter-Play: Timebox the trial period and tie it to retroactive compensation.
    The Script: "I welcome the opportunity to drive these senior deliverables. Standard operational practice for interim leadership roles is a 60-day review period. Let's document that upon successful completion of the 60-day milestone on [Date], the formal title and corresponding compensation bracket will be enacted retroactively."
