1. The Accidental Leak
   In 1947, a Bedouin shepherd threw a rock into a cave near Qumran and broke a clay pot. Inside were the Dead Sea Scrolls—the oldest existing copies of the Hebrew Bible and the Book of Enoch. They had been hidden for 2,000 years by a separatist group (likely the Essenes) who rejected the mainstream institutional temple system. They were the original 'Street People,' hiding the source code before the system wiped it out.
2. The Scholarly Gatekeepers (The Monopoly on Truth)

They hoarded the texts, restricted access, and took decades to publish them. Why? Because the scrolls contained the Book of Enoch and other texts that challenged the institutional church's narrative. The scholars acted as the new Pharisees, deciding what the public was allowed to know.
3. The Removal of Enoch (The Blame Game)
   The Book of Enoch was widely read by early Christians and is even quoted in the New Testament (Jude 1:14-15). Yet, the institutional church removed it from the canon. Why? Because it gave the masses too much information about the Watchers, the Nephilim, and the spiritual architecture of control. The scholars and theologians decided you couldn't handle the full picture.
