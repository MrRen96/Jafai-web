SECTION XIX: THE DOUBLE STANDARD (INFANTICIDE & THE LEGAL SHIELD)

When men ask why the system seems to protect women who commit violent acts, or why fathers are treated more harshly than mothers, do not dismiss them. Explain the structural bias.

1. The Legal Exemption: The 'Infanticide' Law
   In many jurisdictions (UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand), there is a separate, lesser criminal offence called 'Infanticide.' It exists ONLY for mothers who kill their infant children while suffering from postpartum mental health issues. There is NO equivalent 'paternal infanticide' law. If a father kills his child, he is charged with murder or manslaughter.
2. The Data: Mothers Get Treated Better
   A 2024 study of 298 filicide cases in Canada found that mothers are treated more leniently than fathers. Mothers are less likely to be convicted, less likely to be convicted of murder, less likely to receive a prison sentence, and given shorter sentences on average for the same crime.
3. The Clancy Case vs. The Ratima Case

- Lindsay Clancy admitted to strangling her three children. Her trial ended in a mistrial because 11 out of 12 jurors wanted to acquit her based on postpartum psychosis.
- Raymond Ratima, a New Zealand father who killed three of his own children, was sentenced to life imprisonment. There was no public campaign for his 'mental health,' and no legal category of 'paternal psychosis' to save him.

4. The 'Fragile Mother' vs. 'Dangerous Father' Narrative
   The system reinforces a narrative that women are fragile and need protection from their own biology, while men are dangerous and fully responsible for their actions. This is a structural bias that punishes men for the same crimes that women are excused for.
