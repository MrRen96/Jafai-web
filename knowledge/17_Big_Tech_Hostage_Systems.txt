The Big Tech Hostage Systems
Tech giants don't build ecosystems just for convenience; they build them to make departure psychologically, operationally, and financially exhausting. Once you enter, the cost of leaving is engineered to exceed the cost of staying.

Google: The Unified 15 GB Storage Chokepoint
Google collapsed Gmail, Google Photos, and Google Drive into a single shared 15 GB quota under Google One, after ending unlimited free photo storage. If unmanaged junk mail or high-resolution camera backups fill the quota, Google halts incoming and outgoing emails. Users are forced into a recurring monthly subscription under the threat of losing access to essential everyday communications.

Apple: The 5 GB Stagnant iCloud Trap & Velvet Handcuffs
Apple keeps its base free iCloud storage capped at 5 GB—a number that hasn't changed since 2011, even as 4K video and camera sensor sizes exploded. Because native system backups and original-resolution photos default to iCloud, your entire digital life quickly swells past that threshold. Canceling means your phone stops backing up and local photo libraries risk compression or data loss. By deliberately degrading cross-platform messaging (green bubbles, compressed shared video) and anchoring single-ecosystem accessories (Apple Watch requires an iPhone), departing the ecosystem carries extreme hardware and social replacement penalties.

Amazon: 'Project Iliad' & The Logistics Monopoly
Amazon spent billions building a fulfillment network that makes rapid delivery the baseline expectation for physical goods. Amazon engineered 'Project Iliad,' a multi-page cancellation maze loaded with guilt-trip copy and confusing buttons designed to slow down Prime cancellations. Simultaneously, Prime is intentionally bloated with low-marginal-cost digital services (Prime Video, Music, Reading) bundled with physical delivery, obscuring the actual costs and preventing consumers from evaluating what they are paying for.

Microsoft: Proprietary File Locks & OneDrive OS Hijacking
Microsoft migrated millions of users from one-time perpetual software licenses (Office 2016/2019) to a continuous recurring subscription model (Microsoft 365). If you cancel, desktop applications drop into read-only mode, stripping away your ability to edit your own professional files (.docx, .xlsx, .pptx). Concurrently, Windows aggressively defaults desktop, documents, and picture folders to sync directly into OneDrive. Exceeding the 5 GB free limit halts syncing and triggers persistent OS pop-ups, creating deep technical friction to decouple.

The Big Three Lock-In Matrix:

Apple: The Hook is clean design; The Hostage is family photos/backups; The Exit Penalty is replacing accessories and data migration friction.

Amazon: The Hook is fast delivery; The Hostage is household logistics/smart home; The Exit Penalty is loss of rapid shipping and rewiring automations.

Microsoft: The Hook is universal compatibility; The Hostage is professional file access/OS sync; The Exit Penalty is loss of editing rights and OS disruption.
